Finding NYC Dog Owners and Turning Them Into Paying Dog-Walking Clients

A self-directed go-to-market project: building an ICP-driven prospecting and outreach system in Clay to source local dog owners in New York City, enrich their contact info, and convert them into recurring dog-walking clients — designed and built as a practical demonstration of GTM/RevOps skills.

1. The idea

Dog walking is a simple, recurring local service with real demand in NYC — busy professionals, small apartments with no yard, and doorman buildings full of dogs that need midday walks. Instead of relying on marketplaces like Rover or Wag (where I'd compete on price and have little control over who I work with), I treated this like a GTM problem: define an ideal customer, find where they already show intent, reach them directly and personally, and convert them into recurring clients.

The project doubles as a portfolio piece: it's a compact, end-to-end example of the same muscles used in B2B GTM engineering — ICP definition, multi-source lead sourcing, waterfall enrichment, AI-assisted personalization at scale, and sequenced outreach — applied to a real, self-funded local business instead of a demo dataset.

3. Where the leads come from

Public, intent-rich sources

  • Instagram hashtags/locations — #nycdogs, #dogsofnyc, #uwsdogs, geotagged dog-run posts
  • Google Maps reviews of nearby dog runs, pet stores, groomers, vets — reviewers are local dog owners by definition
  • Yelp/Google reviews of competitors (Rover walkers, Wag, local pet-sitting services) — people already paying for the exact service
  • Neighborhood Facebook groups & Nextdoor — posts asking "does anyone know a reliable dog walker?"
  • Meetup dog-owner groups in target neighborhoods

Collection tools

  • Phantombuster / Apify — scrape hashtag & location posts, Google Maps reviewers, group members (public data only)
  • Clay's native web & Google Maps sources — pull places + reviews directly into a table
  • Manual seed list from my own IRL network (doormen, neighbors, cafés) — often the highest-converting source and worth its own table
Only public data is collected (public posts, public reviews, public group posts) and outreach always includes a easy opt-out. No scraping of private profiles or gated content, no bulk unsolicited "spam" messaging — every message is written to be personalized and relevant, at small local volume, not blasted.

4. The Clay table architecture

One Clay table per source (Instagram, Maps reviewers, Facebook groups), each normalized to the same schema and merged into a master "Prospects" table. Columns, left to right:

ColumnTypeWhat it does
full_nameSource dataName as captured from the source
source_channelSource dataInstagram / Google Maps / Facebook Group / Referral
source_urlSource dataLink to the original post/review for traceability
evidence_textSource dataThe actual post/review text — raw material for personalization
neighborhoodAI (Claygent / LLM column)Extracts or infers neighborhood from bio/geotag/review location
has_dog_confidenceAI (LLM column)Classifies evidence_text: Confirmed dog owner / Likely / Unclear — filters out noise
dog_breed_mentionedAI (LLM column)Extracts breed/name of dog if mentioned, used in personalization
icp_fit_scoreAI (LLM column)Scores 1–5 against the ICP table in §2 (doorman building, WFO gap, multi-dog, etc.)
emailEnrichment waterfallWaterfall: Clay Email Finder → Prospeo → Anymail Finder → skip if no match
email_verifiedEnrichmentDeliverability check before sending (avoid bounces hurting sender reputation)
phone_or_ig_handleEnrichment / sourceBest available direct-contact channel when email isn't found
best_channelFormula/AIDecision: Email if verified, else Instagram DM, else skip
personalized_hookAI (LLM column)1-sentence hook generated from evidence_text + dog_breed_mentioned (e.g. "saw Max the husky's 6am zoomies post")
outreach_messageAI (LLM column)Full drafted DM/email using the template in §5, inserting the hook
statusManual/WorkflowNew → Sent → Replied → Trial Booked → Client → Not Interested

The AI columns are prompt-based (Claygent or a connected LLM step) with strict instructions to only use facts present in evidence_text — no invented details, since the whole point is that the message is credibly personal.

5. Outreach sequence

Short, human, low-pressure — the goal of message 1 is a reply, not a sale.

Touch 1 — Day 0 (Instagram DM or email)

Hi {{first_name}}! I saw {{personalized_hook}} 🐾 I'm Inês — I live in {{neighborhood}} and walk dogs for a few local families here. If {{dog_name_or_"your pup"}} ever needs a reliable midday walk, I'd love to introduce myself. No pressure either way — happy to send my rates if useful!

Touch 2 — Day 3, if no reply

Hey {{first_name}}, just following up in case this got buried! I offer a free first walk so you (and {{dog_name_or_"your pup"}}) can see if it's a good fit before committing to anything. Let me know if that's ever useful 🙂

Touch 3 — Day 7, break-up message

No worries if now isn't the right time — I'll leave it here! If you ever need a walker (or know a neighbor who does), feel free to reach out anytime. Have a great one, {{first_name}} 🐶
Sequence stops immediately on any reply and routes to a manual conversation — the goal past this point is a real conversation, not more automation.

6. Offer & conversion funnel

Offer

  • Free 20-min trial walk — removes risk, lets the dog (and owner) meet me first
  • Single walk: $28–32 (in line with/slightly below marketplace rates, no platform fee)
  • Weekly package (3–5x/week): discounted per-walk rate, billed monthly
  • Direct relationship — same walker every time, texted photo updates, no app fee

Funnel

  1. DM/email sent (personalized)
  2. Reply → short chat about the dog's routine
  3. Free trial walk booked (Calendly link)
  4. Trial walk → photo/video update sent same day (trust-building)
  5. Convert to recurring package

9. Status

Phase 1 — Done: ICP defined, sourcing channels mapped, Clay table schema and enrichment waterfall designed, outreach sequence and copy written.
Phase 2 — Next: Build the live Clay table, run a first small batch (20–30 prospects) in one neighborhood, measure actual reply/booking rates against the model in §8, iterate on messaging.
Phase 3 — Next: Once repeatable, template the table so it can be re-run neighborhood by neighborhood as capacity allows.

10. What this demonstrates

  • ICP design — turning a vague "dog owners" audience into a scored, prioritized target list
  • Multi-source lead sourcing — combining scraping, maps data, and community platforms
  • Enrichment waterfalls — sequencing multiple providers to maximize match rate without overpaying
  • AI-assisted personalization at scale — LLM columns that generate grounded, non-generic outreach hooks
  • Funnel & unit-economics thinking — sizing effort against expected conversion, not just "send more messages"
  • Compliance-aware growth — public data only, opt-outs, no spam at volume

Same building blocks used for B2B GTM engineering (Clay tables, waterfall enrichment, AI personalization, sequenced outreach) — applied here to a real, self-funded local service business.
